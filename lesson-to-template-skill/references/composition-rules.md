# Composition selection

Use agreed formal markup such as `layout:` first. Free-form notes in the lesson source are provisional production comments, not commands. A note such as `Визуал:` or `Интерактив:` is evidence to consider alongside the actual content structure and density. Template-slide comments and speaker notes describe what an existing composition was designed to hold; verify them against its catalogued named fields and declared capacity.

When the source has no formal format, classify its teaching purpose by meaning rather than by text-field count or formatting. Consider specific meanings first: service/title, true character dialogue, interaction, statistics, comparison, end-of-lesson checklist, and process or development of a situation. Then consider a list, cards, or classification. Use a text composition for theory, explanation, example, production situation, research with conclusion, or problem-explanation-solution when the text does not have a stronger visual structure.

Treat numbered and marked lists as one group. Numbering is a display choice: it does not turn a list into a process, and any ordinary list may be numbered. Keep a checklist separate for an actual self-check or final lesson checklist.

Recognise a process by a temporal, causal, operational, or situation-development relationship. Prefer a timeline, process, or connected-elements composition when showing that relationship is central. A numbered list is a valid fallback when it represents the process more honestly at the available density. Do not use a process composition for an ordinary list solely because its points are ordered or numbered.

Use an introduction-and-cards composition only when the source already consists of a short introduction and several independent, homogeneous elements. Do not fragment a coherent explanatory paragraph into cards merely to use their available fields.

Before choosing a candidate, estimate the density of the source: the length and number of independent blocks, their roles, and the room suggested by the template brief, named fields, and catalogued capacity where available. Use that estimate to rule out plainly unsuitable variants within the semantically correct group. For `staging.interaction`, long source text is expected when a planned interaction will contain, reveal, or distribute it.

If no exact composition fits, compare the closest existing compositions and record why each fails. Use title-and-text only when the prose genuinely has no stronger structure. When the prose has a clear key idea that deserves emphasis but the catalog has no suitable emphasis composition, record a template gap for a future template instead of repeatedly flattening such slides into title-and-text. Do not invent a layout ID from an item count. Preserve approved wording, but a small rearrangement of unchanged fragments is allowed when it makes the selected structure clearer; rewriting, shortening, and additions still require explicit approval.

For a true dialogue, use the `staging.blank` exception and always take the first blank slide in the current library. A true dialogue is a speaker-labelled character comment on theory; dialogue wording used inside an example, case, or production situation remains part of that example, case, or situation. Resolve its `image_prompt:` before generation: preserve an author-supplied prompt, or create one from the slide using [dialogue-image-prompt.md](dialogue-image-prompt.md). The returned 16:9 scene includes both Alexey and Denis, even when only one has a spoken line on this slide. The plan records `scenePath` and the speaker-labelled `dialogue` array; the common batch builder inserts the scene as a full-slide image and puts the replicas into the existing editable text field. Do not create a title, speech bubbles, speaker labels, or other overlay objects. Do not search the general catalog or require a separate physical dialogue template.

An exercise question with answer options, sorting, matching, or another explicit exercise mechanic is always interaction staging. Route it directly to the separate physical clone `staging.interaction`, never to the dialogue-only `staging.blank`, and preserve the complete approved source block in its `body` slot. When the lesson explicitly calls for tabs, a marked image, or another interactive mechanic that is not an exercise, decide whether the mechanic makes understanding or practice materially better than an ordinary static slide. Use `staging.interaction` when it does; otherwise select the strongest static composition.

One physical template may support several semantic uses. Record them in `kinds` and keep one stable `templateId`; do not duplicate catalog entries merely to express alternative uses. A composition such as introduction-main-conclusion may therefore also be a temporary fit for situation-actions-result, provided the decision log records that mapping and any missing dedicated template.

For every slide decision, record the chosen composition, intended field mapping, rejected alternatives, the effect of any source comment, confidence, and template gaps. Low confidence or the absence of an honest composition is a stopping condition before PPTX generation. When several valid compositions fit, prefer the compatible variant used least often earlier in the current deck after matching teaching purpose and density.

Capacity is advisory and informs the estimate made before selection. Prefer the composition whose semantic structure matches the material. Do not change font sizes automatically. Record declared capacity risks for manual review and do not replace a comparison of equivalent or parallel dimensions with a good-versus-bad layout solely because its columns are larger.

When a mapped field contains several source run styles, preserve their semantic hierarchy. Put a short term, lead-in, or key conclusion fragment into the accent style and the explanatory remainder into the body style. The selected fragment must carry the main emphasis; do not color or enlarge the entire replacement merely because the first template run is accented.
